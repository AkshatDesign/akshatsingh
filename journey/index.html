03 · UX Trade-Off Log

Six Deliberate Design Decisions

Expand any decision below to see the tension between simplicity and power-user control — and how Journey resolved it.

Why

Indian multi-city trips anchor on flight/train schedules first; hotel nights adapt around arrival times.

Trade-Off

Resort-first planners could feel boxed in by a strict linear wizard.

Resolution

Default to Transport → Stay auto-sync while letting users tap Stay directly in the timeline spine.

Why

Interruptive modals break price comparison rhythm and train users to reflexively dismiss warnings.

Trade-Off

Inline alerts risk being missed without persistent status visibility.

Resolution

Pin a calm conflict counter in the timeline bar; only Red blocking errors halt final payment.

Why

Many Indian itineraries mix booked hotels with staying at a relative's home or pre-booked villa.

Trade-Off

External stays provide less metadata for check-in conflict checks.

Resolution

One-tap "Staying with friends / Already booked" card keeps the timeline unbroken.

Why

Splitting Flights and Hotels into separate tabs conceals awkward 8-hour landing-to-checkin gaps.

Trade-Off

Long 7-day feeds can feel vertically dense on small phones.

Resolution

Group chronologically by City Block (Goa 3n · Mumbai 2n) with collapsible day rows.

Why

Overnight trains eliminate a hotel night while 6am arrivals demand luggage storage or early check-in.

Trade-Off

Adds edge-case branching to the stay night calculator.

Resolution

Auto-deduct transit nights on sleeper rails and surface one-tap luggage tips on dawn landings.

Why

All-or-nothing ₹40,000+ checkouts trigger hesitation when one hotel choice is still undecided.

Trade-Off

Mixed booked/unbooked states require clear visual differentiation.

Resolution

Allow toggling individual legs at Review while keeping the unified Trip Pass synced.
