01 · Problem Framing

Multi-city trips aren't bigger trips.
They're a different problem.

Most booking experiences are optimized for simple one-way or round-trip journeys. Multi-city travel introduces a fundamentally different planning problem, one current solutions patch rather than solve.

How tools work today

  • Each leg is an isolated transaction, no intelligence between decisions.
  • Conflicts are discovered at checkout, when it's too late.
  • Users do the mental math manually across tabs and spreadsheets.
  • Hotel dates are typed by hand and disconnected from transport.
  • Total cost is a surprise revealed at the very end.

How this design works

  • The trip is one connected journey, every decision informs the next.
  • Conflicts are caught in real time, inline, the moment they appear.
  • The app does the mental math: dates, gaps, and timing.
  • Hotel check-in auto-populates from when the transport actually lands.
  • A persistent cost bar keeps value visible at every step.
How Might I

How might I design a multi-destination travel experience where complexity is managed by the system, so the user only ever feels the journey, not the friction?

02 · User Mental Model

Three layers of how people actually think about a trip.

If the interface mirrors the user's mental model, complexity disappears. I mapped three layers, and let each one dictate a design decision.

Layer
01

The Journey, they think in legs, not bookings

Users visualize a linear path with stops. They need to see the whole route before filling in any of the parts.

Design implication: Always show the complete route as a persistent visual anchor, from the first screen onward.
Layer
02

The Stay, they think in nights, not dates

"3 nights in Goa" feels natural. But accommodation is sandwiched between two transport decisions users don't consciously connect.

Design implication: Auto-populate hotel dates from transport times. Show the night count prominently. Flag conflicts immediately.
Layer
03

The Time, they run constant mental simulations

"Will the flight land in time? Does checkout happen before departure?" This is where anxiety peaks.

Design implication: A timeline view is essential, not optional. Show time gaps, flag tight windows, surface conflicts early.
EMOTIONAL STATE · SETUP

Excitement

During setup they need speed and a sense of possibility.

EMOTIONAL STATE · MID-PLAN

Anxiety

Mid-planning they need clarity and guidance.

EMOTIONAL STATE · REVIEW

Confidence

At review they need completeness and control.

03 · User Scenarios

Three travellers, three very different needs.

The design has to serve the nervous first-timer and the seasoned pro in the same flow.

P

Priya, 28

First multi-city trip
"I don't know where to start. Do I book flights first or hotels? What if something doesn't line up?"
Needs guidance & structure

The app must protect her from mistakes she can't anticipate.

RN

Rahul & Neha, 34

Family of 3, with a toddler
"We need a hotel near the beach, flights with a reasonable layover, and to check out before noon everywhere for the baby's naps."
Need flexibility within structure

Specific constraints standard flows don't accommodate.

K

Kiran, 31

Frequent business traveller
"I've already got my flights sorted. I just need to add hotels and see everything in one place."
Needs speed & control

The guided flow should never slow him down.

04 · Approach & Reasoning

Six principles that do the heavy lifting.

Each principle resolves a specific source of friction for a specific user.

P01

Journey First, Booking Second

The complete route BLR → GOA → MUM → BLR is visible from the first screen and persists throughout. Priya's anxiety drops the moment she can see the whole trip.

P02

One Leg At A Time

Planning three legs at once is overwhelming. Guide through each leg sequentially, transport, stay, activities, then move forward. Completion keeps momentum.

P03

Connected Decisions

A flight landing at 3pm auto-sets check-in to 3pm. A 6am train flags a checkout conflict. The output of one decision becomes the input of the next.

P04

Real-Time Conflict Detection

Three severity tiers, red blocks, yellow warns, blue suggests. Only truly critical issues interrupt flow. This stops alarm fatigue without hiding real problems.

P05

Escape Hatches For Power Users

Every guided step has an "I already have this booked" option. Kiran enters a reference and skips the search. Lower the floor without lowering the ceiling.

P06

Price In Context, Not Isolation

A persistent cost bar updates with each selection. Smart comparisons surface: "₹500 more but saves a hotel night, net saving ₹1,200." Value, not just numbers.

The Overarching Tension

The design defaults to guided and safe for Priya, but always offers escape hatches for flexible, powerful use by Kiran. Lower the floor for beginners, without lowering the ceiling for experts.

06 · Key Decisions & Trade-offs

Six decisions, each with a cost.

Every choice traded something away. Tap a decision to see the reasoning, the trade-off, and how it was resolved.

Why
Multi-destination trips have hard dependencies, accommodation dates depend on transport times. Free-form invites mistakes.
Trade-off
Linear flows can feel slow and restrictive to experienced users.
Resolution
Linear by default, with an "I already have this booked" escape hatch at every step.
User benefit
Priya gets protection. Kiran gets speed.
Why
Conflicts discovered at the review stage cause abandonment, the user has to unwind decisions.
Trade-off
Too many warnings causes alarm fatigue; users stop reading them.
Resolution
A three-tier severity system. Only red blocks progress; yellow and blue inform without interrupting.
User benefit
Problems surface early, while they're still cheap to fix.
Why
Connecting transport times to hotel dates is the single biggest source of multi-city booking errors.
Trade-off
Auto-dates might not match the user's actual intent.
Resolution
Auto-populate with transparent reasoning shown ("Based on your 11pm arrival") plus one-tap override.
User benefit
The error-prone math is done for them, but they stay in control.
Why
Multi-destination trips are fundamentally about time. Card views break temporal continuity.
Trade-off
A full timeline is dense on a small mobile screen.
Resolution
Collapsible days with a skeleton view by default; expand any day for detail.
User benefit
The whole journey at a glance, depth on demand.
Why
Activities complete the itinerary, without them, users leave to plan elsewhere.
Trade-off
Adds steps and complexity to an already multi-step flow.
Resolution
Activities are optional, always last in each leg's flow, and never block progress.
User benefit
A richer trip for those who want it, zero drag for those who don't.
Why
Revealing total cost only at checkout causes sticker shock and abandonment.
Trade-off
Constant price visibility can anchor users on cost and discourage good choices.
Resolution
A persistent, non-intrusive cost bar plus smart value comparisons surfaced contextually.
User benefit
No surprises at the end; value framed in context, not in isolation.
07 · Edge Cases

The hard parts a real trip throws at you.

Robustness lives in the edges. Each case is handled by an existing system behaviour, not a one-off patch.

In transit

Overnight travel

A night train spans two calendar days, no hotel needed.

HandlingAn "in transit" state fills the gap on the timeline so there's no confusing empty night.
Blue suggestion

Luggage gap

Checkout at 11am, flight at 7pm, an 8-hour gap with bags.

Handling"Store your luggage nearby" suggestion with integrated storage options.
Single leg

Connecting flights

A flight with a layover is one decision, not two.

HandlingTreated as a single leg with expandable layover details. The transit city never triggers an accommodation step.
Set once, applied everywhere

Traveller-specific rules

Infants don't need separate train seats; children need specific fare categories.

HandlingTraveller composition is set once at trip creation and flows through every search automatically.
Yellow warning

Tight transfer window

75 minutes between arrival and the next departure.

HandlingFlagged inline. The user can proceed but is informed, the decision stays theirs, made consciously.
Blue suggestion

Early arrival, late checkout

Flight lands 6am, hotel check-in is 2pm.

Handling"Early check-in may not be available, book the previous night or consider day-use options."
Red · Blocks

Departure before checkout, or overlapping dates. Must be resolved before proceeding.

Yellow · Warns

Under 90 min between arrival and departure. Flagged, but never blocks.

Blue · Suggests

Luggage storage, gap nights, late checkout. Informational and dismissable.
